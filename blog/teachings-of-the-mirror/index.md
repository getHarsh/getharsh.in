# Teachings of the mirror

*A reflection written for Mahashivratri in February 2020.*

There is a saying that the teacher appears when the student is ready. There is another that there is no knowledge without a teacher. What interests me is that the teacher can be formless. A person can teach, but so can an encounter that refuses to fit what I already believe. That was one of the largest things the preceding decade had taught me.

At the time, I looked forward to a day when I could retire as a professor. Every wakeful moment felt like part of becoming a good teacher. Before that, though, I had so much to learn and do. I did not want to hand future students a half-baked perception of reality. I wanted to bring them something factual, experiential and broad enough to have survived contact with more than my own explanation.

It was a long journey. I was in no rush.

<a id="a-festival-and-a-teacher"></a>

## A festival, and a teacher

Mahashivratri and Gurupurnima were the festivals I felt closest to. On Mahashivratri, alongside other devotees, I celebrated Shiva and his beauty. In the yogic legends, Shiva is Adiyogi, the first yogi and a great teacher. The figure drew my attention towards what a teacher might make possible.

Now, what does any of that have to do with a mirror? Mere ramblings of a madman, or an underlying symmetry? Of course I had a reason for going there.

A true teacher, in the ideal I was reaching for, is an unbiased observer who gives you methods for discarding the notions that hold you down. The teacher does not simply put a more impressive set of notions in their place. They help you see the choices that your account of yourself and society had made invisible. Like a mirror, they show you something you have to encounter for yourself.

The teacher can help reveal the path. Walking it remains your own doing. That was the meaning I found in Shiva's bhakti: a relationship that could liberate the seeker into discovery, while leaving the work of living with the seeker.

<a id="methods-in-the-middle-of-the-chaos"></a>

## Methods in the middle of the chaos

Amid the chaos, I found myself reaching for methods rather than a rule for every possible situation. You follow a practice. Distractions and hurdles arrive. Your picture of reality changes as you make your way through it. Faith can keep you moving when the path is no longer as obvious as it looked from the beginning.

The mirror remains beside you. What it shows may be uncomfortable. There is no guarantee that the person you meet in it resembles the person you have been explaining to everyone else. The choice to respond to that encounter is yours. In this image, the methods do not change to flatter the person I would prefer to see. I am the one being asked to change.

That is a demanding image of teaching. It asks for more than passing on an answer. It asks whether I can help somebody examine what stands between them and their own understanding. It also turns the question back on me: what am I still unable to see because I need my present account to remain intact?

<a id="the-difficulty-of-being-the-mirror"></a>

## The difficulty of being the mirror

In ordinary life, I am hardly an unbiased observer. I have more biases than I can count and much to learn. Calling the teacher a mirror does not suddenly make me one. It makes the distance between the ideal and my own practice harder to overlook.

What I kept finding in the teachings of Shiva was an image of balance: an unbiased consciousness pervading existence. I found it almost poetic. Survival and exuberance are different ambitions. The habits that get us through a day may not be the habits that let us encounter it fully.

I did not know what God was, or whether God existed at all. I was still discovering things with every wakeful moment. Yet the idea of a supreme consciousness that could remain balanced and unbiased gave me something to contemplate. Seen through that ideal, the austerity associated with Shiva's path began to make sense to me. The simplest statement could contain an extraordinarily difficult way of living.

<a id="back-to-the-hazy-borders"></a>

## Back to the hazy borders

I returned to the borders between history, science, art, myths and legends. The festival gave me an occasion to stay with the questions, and to be grateful for the teachers I had already encountered. Their forms had been different. So had the things they made possible.

The aspiration to teach belonged to that moment in my life. The question it carried continues through [learning with a real problem](https://getharsh.in/collections/learning-and-practice/a-real-problem-is-a-better-learning-brief/): what must I understand, and what support must I offer, before another person can make the work their own?

A mirror cannot take the journey for the person standing before it. It can make the next act of looking possible.

And be careful what you wish for. It just might come true. 😉
