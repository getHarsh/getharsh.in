# Sneak peek into infinity: the breath of cosmos and its duration

In the quiet moments of deep breathing, have you ever felt a subtle shift in your perception of time and reality? This experience is where I want to begin an inquiry into consciousness, reaching beyond the scale of an individual life.

It begins with a breath.

In our daily lives, breathing is an automatic function, a simple necessity of life. But when we attend to it, especially through practices such as pranayama, something ordinarily unnoticed becomes available to us. We can change the rhythm and notice how the encounter changes. It is not only meditative mumbo-jumbo: breathing practices are also used to help bring about a relaxation response.<sup><a id="fnref-note-627265617468696e67"></a>[1](#fn-note-627265617468696e67)</sup> What interests me here is the connection between a physical process, our state of mind and the way time is experienced.

<a id="give-the-breath-an-input"></a>

## Give the breath an input

An engineer reaches for a model. Perhaps the duration and quality of a breath can be treated as a control signal, with a transfer function representing the process through which breathing influences consciousness. Something like

$$
Y(t)=T\cdot X(t),
$$

where $X(t)$ represents the breathing pattern, $Y(t)$ the consciousness level we would like to describe, and $T$ the relation between them. The simplest version makes $T=k$, a positive constant: change the input and the output changes proportionally.

Someone with a neuroscience background could model the mechanics better than I can. I clearly do not have that background. The mind is non-linear and considerably more complicated than this constant gain; even the quantities would need research before we could measure the relationship. For now, the sketch gives me a way to ask how a physical breathing pattern might influence consciousness.

If you are particularly curious about the time variable, much like I am, the question soon becomes larger than this little equation. How do we describe all the different ways an interval can be encountered? What might a vocabulary for that question let us see? That curiosity also runs through [my questions about geometry and description](https://getharsh.in/blog/an-analogy-can-start-an-argument/).

But you get the general idea of what I am trying to communicate here, right? I digress. Coming back: a change in breathing is an ordinary physical act through which we can examine a change in experience. I want to follow that connection into a much less ordinary scale.

<a id="it-expands-to-cosmic-scales"></a>

## It expands to cosmic scales

Begin with the human scale: each breath, each moment. Seconds become minutes, hours become days, and years open into yugas, mahayugas, manvantaras and kalpas. These names belong to a larger account of recurrence, eventually taking us to the life of Brahmā and the world associated with it.

Now meet the image of Mahā-Vishnu's breath. In the *Brahma-saṁhitā*, the lives of the Brahmās presiding over universes fall within one exhalation.<sup><a id="fnref-note-636f736d69632d627265617468"></a>[2](#fn-note-636f736d69632d627265617468)</sup> A universe's entire history becomes part of another rhythm. Its creation and dissolution fit within a breath; another breath opens the possibility again.

Tale, wisdom, fiction, interpretation: whatever your angle, the image asks something rather startling of our attention. One breath contains a lifetime of worlds.

Where, in this grand scale of breath, are you? Let us choose seven seconds for a human breath. It is an arbitrary interval for the thought experiment. How many such intervals belong within the cosmic breath? What fraction of the larger duration does your breath occupy?

For any specified finite duration, we can divide by seven seconds and obtain a finite count. That is one calculation. The thought I want to follow goes further: take the cosmic scale as unbounded. There is no largest duration in the comparison I am making. The names can be variables for you; the question is the relation between an interval we can hold and a scale without a final bound.

<a id="how-many-zeroes-are-contained-in-an-infinity"></a>

## How many zeroes are contained in an infinity?

How many twos are there in ten? Five. How many ones? Ten.

$$
\frac{10}{2}=5,\qquad \frac{10}{1}=10.
$$

Now ask the deliberately awkward question: how many zeroes are there in an infinity?

Your intuition probably says infinitely many. Trust your intuition, then look at what we have actually put into the fraction. The human breath was never zero. We chose seven seconds. It only began to *look* like zero when placed beside an ever-larger duration. And infinity is not a very large real number waiting to be divided; it names the absence of a bound.

Write $B$ for the duration being compared, measured in seconds. The number of seven-second intervals is $B/7$. As the duration grows without bound,

$$
\lim_{B\to\infty}\frac{B}{7}=\infty.
$$

Look in the other direction and the fraction represented by one human breath tends to zero:

$$
\lim_{B\to\infty}\frac{7}{B}=0.
$$

It is no longer an indeterminate form. The denominator is finite and nonzero; the duration in the numerator grows without bound. The answer in this construction is infinity.

<a id="what-would-that-mean"></a>

## What would that mean?

With breath and time as proxies for consciousness in this thought experiment, I picture consciousness expanding towards infinity as the imagined breath extends towards the cosmic scale. Looking back towards the human scale, our individual awareness becomes a near-zero part of that expanse. The ratio gives me the scale; what follows is the possibility I see in it.

Within that picture, cosmic consciousness can be a superset of individual experiences: all forms of life, all the different encounters through which existence becomes known. Our awareness is a drop in that ocean, but a drop belongs to the ocean. Being small in the comparison has not placed it outside the whole.

You know the monkey typing on a keyboard for long enough to produce *Hamlet*? That is the image I reach for: an immense space of possibilities in which something as particular as a play can occur.<sup><a id="fnref-note-68616d6c6574"></a>[3](#fn-note-68616d6c6574)</sup> A cosmic or collective consciousness could contain the individual forms through which we encounter it. The superset, or universal set, if you will.

But calculating the ratio and experiencing that connection are different things. [Knowing, understanding and realising](https://getharsh.in/blog/knowing-understanding-and-realising/) can take us through the same words and leave us somewhere quite different.

My early generative-art explorations for [Sleepwalker](https://sleepwalker.in/) gave this question another form. An image of Markandeya could hold the encounter with another scale while I worked through the calculation separately. It helps me separate the myth from the maths. The [creative inquiry that grows from the breath](https://sleepwalker.in/blog/the-world-inside-a-breath/) has room for a witness, a world and the bewilderment that a ratio cannot draw for us.

<a id="the-universe-in-our-breath"></a>

## The universe in our breath

The attraction of this idea is not only the size of the number. It is the possibility of meeting something immense through an act so ordinary that I usually do not notice it. The traditions of breath and meditation invite that encounter from experience; the mathematical construction gives one relation within it a form we can examine.

I see in it a connection between individual awareness and an unbounded possibility of consciousness. A small experience can remain intimate while belonging to something beyond the frame in which I first understood it. The cosmic scale does not make the breath disappear. It changes what I can imagine the breath participating in.

So, take a deep breath and remember: it begins with a breath.
