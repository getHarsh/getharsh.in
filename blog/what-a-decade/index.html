What a decade! Makes me wonder what is to come

On this page
  1. Rule 32: enjoy the little things
  2. What matters changes as I follow the thought
  3. When thoughts meet the soul
  4. What will we create?

A reflection from December 2019, looking back over the 2010s.

2010. Failure first got to me. I was in class eleven.

Failure presents a choice, although the choice depends on the circumstances. I can stay where I am, trying to escape the failure, or find another path towards what I wanted. Perhaps the encounter makes me see the situation clearly for the first time. I had seen a fair amount of failure in the years that followed. What hurt most was often the greed to have success without any of it.

That becomes more relevant as the thing I want to create becomes larger. But the decade was not merely an account of successes and failures. It was memories, experiences, people and ideas. It was life.

Ferris Bueller's reminder to stop and look around had stayed with me. Life was perfectly capable of moving on while I was busy preparing the account of it.

Rule 32: enjoy the little things

Somewhere in the middle of the decade, things became particularly interesting. Zombieland supplied the plainer version of Bueller's advice: rule 32, “Enjoy the little things.” My favourite rule. At the end of 2019, I still had not watched the sequel. It had a place on the list, and I was looking forward to it.

The little things mattered partly because I was rather good at making the questions enormous. A couple of weeks before writing this, I had followed a train of thought through meditation, creativity, realisation, hallucination and existence. Following the thought was one capacity; keeping the distinctions clear was another. Somewhere among those hazy borders, I could lose the thread I had set out to understand.

That would not make me a good teacher. Clearing the haze was supposed to be the job, not adding another impressive-looking cloud to it. I needed to work on the difference. I suspected I would need rule 32 even more in the coming decade than I had in this one.

What matters changes as I follow the thought

I often follow a thought well beyond the place it first seemed useful. As I do, things I expected to matter do not always matter in the same way. Other things, initially peripheral, become difficult to ignore. There are outliers. There are always outliers.

I wanted to be aware of what happened to me and why, without letting every event determine the entirety of my response. That is an ambition, and sometimes a privilege. Writing it down does not make it easy. Someone once told me never to underestimate nature. I try not to. A reflex can respond before I have assembled a sophisticated explanation of what needs a response.

Then the questions grow again. Maybe nothing matters. Maybe everything does. Wait. Where was I?

The more I followed them, the less the decade resembled a progression towards fewer doubts. I had more questions than when it began. Existence and experience had once seemed easier to hold apart. The world had seemed simpler. That simplicity had been useful, but I was no longer sure it had been understanding.

When thoughts meet the soul

One thought kept returning in a more lyrical form:

When thoughts meet the soul, it becomes an art. When art meets sound, it becomes music. When music meets science, it becomes a mantra.

An artist pours something of themselves into the work. If only God can create, as the saying goes, what does it mean that people create every day? Did God create a soulmate to show us the science, or did we create science to see the art? Perhaps God poured a soul into science. Perhaps God is an art.

I put it under a little declaration: #MusicIsJustMyMantra. The inquiry now has other forms in thinking across images, sound and sequence. The earlier words still carry something of the person asking the question before those later forms existed.

What will we create?

That was what made the next decade interesting to me. Not merely what would happen, but what we would create within it. Which questions would survive? Which relationships would make them worth pursuing? What would become of the things we had not yet learned to notice?

I hoped Bueller would stay beside us for as long as we needed the reminder. Stop. Look around. There may be a life happening somewhere outside the plan.

There was no other way but to be okay. The question was how soon I wanted to begin. ¯_(ツ)_/¯
