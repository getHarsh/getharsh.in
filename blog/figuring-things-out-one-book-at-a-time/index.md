# Figuring things out, one book at a time

*From February 2020: a pause, a new place and a great many unread books.*

In hindsight, there is no other way but to be okay. Some experiences define you; some refine you. All of them teach you something, even when you would rather have received the lesson in another form.

I kept returning to the idea of choosing love over hatred. It led me towards forgiveness, and towards a question I had not dealt with as patiently as I imagined: what about forgiving myself before trying to forgive what had wronged me?

I had only skimmed the *Heart Sutra*. I was reaching for a connection, not claiming to have understood the text. There was still a book to read, a life to examine and a considerable distance between recognising an appealing sentence and knowing what to do with it.

<a id="when-the-remedy-becomes-another-injury"></a>

## When the remedy becomes another injury

How far can I go to fix something that seems broken? Until the remedy becomes worse than the disease? Until the broken thing consumes the person trying to repair it?

Straightforward questions, slippery ground. Before deciding how far to go, I had to ask why I felt compelled to fix it. Why this attachment? Why this insistence on deciding that something was broken, and that I was the person who needed to make it whole?

The answer I could reach was a willingness to conform to what I loved: a person, a relationship, an idea or the self I wanted to maintain. Love could have many forms. It could be directed towards a partner, a teacher, parents, friends or family; it need not have a person or even a form as its object. What I called true love asked for something less possessive than making the object agree with my idea of it.

That was easy to write. Living it was another matter.

<a id="acceptance-and-forgiveness"></a>

## Acceptance and forgiveness

Straightforward is often mistaken for easy. The two are not reliably correlated, let alone causally joined.

I wanted to get beyond the exhausting court in my head: right and wrong, good and evil, fair and unfair. Yet I could not simply float above it. First I had to distinguish accepting what had happened from forgiving it, and both from approving it. A fact could be undeniable while my relationship to it remained unsettled.

I found attachment to identity at the centre of that difficulty. I worked hard to maintain an account of myself. Every wound then threatened more than the immediate relationship; it threatened the account. Explaining myself again could become another attempt to make the other person preserve it for me.

There was a difference between closing my heart to stop the pain and forgiving so that the pain could fade. Neither arrived because I had found a sufficiently neat paragraph about it. I was trying to understand what I could release without pretending that the experience had meant nothing.

People remember how an encounter felt as well as what was said. Another explanation is not always another kindness. That was a difficult lesson for somebody who kept reaching for words.

<a id="the-choices-inside-the-grand-explanation"></a>

## The choices inside the grand explanation

Why bring trouble upon ourselves when life is already complicated? Why press the self-destruct button? A television monologue can make the question funny, or at least recognisable: we keep doing the thing that hurts, then celebrate the relief when it stops.

The image I kept from *The Matrix* was Morpheus showing Neo a path he could not walk for him. Yes, another reference. But it got at something I needed: somebody could offer an insight, and I could still have to choose what to do with it.

Add self-obsession, a little narcissism and a healthy dose of the God complex, then look again. The grand account of existence dissolves into small choices. What do I do next? Which response do I repeat? What do I let another person be without requiring them to support my explanation of myself?

Those easily overlooked choices mattered to the pursuit of happiness more than the size of the theory I built around them.

<a id="the-family-i-discovered"></a>

## The family I discovered

I was grateful for the friends and family who had stayed beside me. I had not thought very hard about friendship before. I assumed it was mostly a matter of choice. Looking back, it felt more like something we had discovered over time: neither person could simply appoint the other to the role and make it true.

Living away from our families, I had not noticed exactly when friends became family. I was lucky. And, as a sitcom mother would remind us, families are embarrassing. If they never embarrass you, have they really completed the qualification?

At that moment in 2020, I was taking time off to work out the next part of my journey. A new place, the same family and extraordinary friends. The books, at least, were in abundant supply.

So I decided it was time to forgive, and to keep figuring things out one book at a time. The later [question of detachment](https://getharsh.in/blog/holding-an-idea-without-becoming-it/) would take another form. Here, it began much closer to home: what could I stop demanding from a hurt, so that the rest of life could continue?
