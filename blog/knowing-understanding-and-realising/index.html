Knowing, understanding and realising

On this page
  1. The useful distance of an abstraction
  2. Understanding can become a hiding place
  3. When the explanation comes back to me
  4. Murti, darshan and the missing translation

Suppose, for a thought experiment, I tell you that a thousand children die every day for want of clean water. It is a terrible number, but a number you can put down and continue with your day. Now imagine those thousand bodies on the road you take home. Then hold that scene across ten years: ten times 365 times a thousand. Finally, imagine recognising somebody you love among them. The information has acquired a relation to your own life. What happened to its meaning?

Knowing, understanding, realising. We use the words almost interchangeably, yet there is a whole distance between those encounters. I can repeat an accurate explanation and remain strangely untouched by it. Another encounter can make that distance impossible to maintain. I am trying to find words for the change, not a universal staircase everyone climbs in the same order.

The useful distance of an abstraction

Knowing can be accurate, useful and uneventful. I can remember a number, repeat a definition or recognise a pattern without having a profound experience. Much ordinary work depends on precisely that ability. An engineer needs to reason about a plant without experiencing every consequence of every component all at once.

But an abstraction gives us something by leaving something else out. A map can let me find a place without giving me its life. A description of a ritual can name its sequence without telling me what participating means to someone. I can read an explanation of a passage and retain its conclusion while missing the question that made the passage important. Each representation makes a particular encounter possible. Its usefulness can also make the omitted part easy to forget.

The omitted part sometimes changes the decision. A command that looks successful on a screen matters because equipment and people depend on the physical result. A resolved ticket matters because a person was trying to finish something. Recovering that context is not sentiment added after the technical work; it can change what the technical work is for. In another setting, returning to a familiar book with an unfamiliar concern can change which passage holds my attention. The material did not need to change for my relationship to it to become different.

Understanding can become a hiding place

Understanding gives the fact a structure. Why did the temperature change? What happens if the pump is turned down? Why does a passage return to an image that seemed incidental on the first reading? How does the order of a story change what I inferred from it? An explanation lets us predict, compare and identify missing connections instead of merely repeating what we have heard. The relation gives the remembered fact somewhere to work.

Yet an elegant explanation can become a place to hide. I can explain how an organisation overwhelms its team and still reproduce the pattern. I can describe what makes a product difficult while continuing to value it mainly for its sophistication. Understanding the arrangement does not automatically settle my relationship to it: which costs I accept, which consequences I overlook, what I am willing to change.

By realising, I mean the point at which that relationship becomes harder to evade. The meaning is no longer merely an object I can describe. I am implicated in it. Emotion may accompany the change, but intensity is not what makes the interpretation true. A vivid experience can mislead as well as illuminate. The important movement is from holding a description to encountering what the description means in a life.

When the explanation comes back to me

Smart Joules and Zenoti brought technical systems and the people using them into focus for me in different ways. Components communicating mattered, but so did what an operator could understand and do with the result. A roadmap mattered, but so did the customer trying to run a business rather than navigate the product. The technical account becomes stronger when it can recognise those purposes.

Realisation need not produce an immediate visible action. A person may need time to change a habit, may lack the power to act or may discover a conflict they cannot yet resolve. An action can express an inward change without measuring all of it. Turning realising into doing would lose the very difference I am trying to name.

That distinction also makes room for patience with others. Explaining something more clearly may help them understand it without giving them my relationship to it. They have their own history, responsibilities and conditions for acting. Repetition alone cannot make those differences disappear.

Murti, darshan and the missing translation

The distinction draws me towards darshan and the experience of a murti. Translating the latter simply as “idol” can leave out the encounter I am interested in: how meeting a form might become something other than knowing its description. The person brings familiarity, practice, expectation and history; two people can stand before the same form and have different encounters. I am interested in that accumulated relationship, in how a form can hold more than the particulars a stranger sees at first glance. The visible object can remain while the encounter changes through a person's life.

I take that difference seriously without making the experience certify every explanation attached to it. Something can be transformative and still invite examination. Meaning is diminished both when we dismiss the encounter as nothing and when we demand that it prove a universal claim.

This is one reason I move between technical explanation, reflective writing and creative forms. A diagram can reveal a dependency. A scene can make its consequence present. A question can expose the assumption that held both together. In Markandeya's encounter, recognising his own hermitage within the impossible world gives its cosmic scale an intimacy that a nested diagram would omit. I can understand the containers without meeting that recognition. The scene gives me another way to approach what the description means.

Writing can make the distance between a statement and its meaning harder to overlook. It need not make every fact emotionally overwhelming. It can recover enough of the world behind an abstraction that our decisions are not made entirely inside it.
