An exploration of causality and awareness in AI, first published in October 2023.

I have been keeping busy following my own advice since I last published. IYKYK ;)

I've set up an experiment across publications, ventures and digital identities. Each gives me a different way to approach the same inquiry: the fundamental nature of causality, and what understanding it could make possible for artificial intelligence. A practical application, a mathematical question and a story can reveal different parts of that problem. The point of connecting them is to let what happens in one change what I ask in another.

Awareness is all you need

The practical starting point is YOJN and DAO Studio's ambition to make AI accessible, usable and available for more people to build with.

“Awareness is All You Need” is the thought I am following. Here, awareness concerns context: how a model is being used, what the task means and why particular training choices or hyperparameters have been selected. Making that setting available to the system could make its response more relevant to the work. This is a proposition about useful context, not a claim that supplying it gives a model conscious experience or settles everything an AI system needs.

The question changes what I want from an application. Producing an answer is one thing; fitting that answer into the situation that gave rise to the question is another. YojnAI gives that inquiry a practical home.

Metadata and meta-awareness

We keep hearing that data is the new oil. Perhaps the metadata is where some of the value is hiding.

Data arrives with circumstances: where it came from, what it describes and what someone intended to do with it. Those circumstances can be lost when everything becomes another training example. My interest in metadata is the possibility of carrying more of that setting into learning, so that the model has a finer account of the task rather than merely more material to process.

That is the sense in which I am exploring meta-awareness. The information about the information becomes part of the question. Whether it helps has to be shown on the task.

Intelligence that can travel

The larger ambition is to commoditise intelligence: make it as accessible and ordinary to use as electricity. Transfer learning offers a route towards that ambition, because learning need not begin from nothing for every application.

The promise is that something learned in one setting can help in another. It is not a guarantee of seamless transfer across arbitrary domains; transferred knowledge can also make the new task worse. The research on negative transfer makes that limit concrete. The interesting work is discovering what can travel and what the receiving task still needs.

The Red Pill follows the economic possibility: what changes when intelligence itself can become something people exchange? The engineering question underneath it is how that reuse could work.

Engineering the unknown

An application gives an abstract idea consequences. Something is built, someone tries to use it, and the result returns information that the original explanation did not contain. That feedback is part of the exploration, not merely the last stage after the thinking is finished.

Sleepwalker, myths and mathematics

Sleepwalker approaches causality through fiction, mythology and scientific ideas. Its protagonist's journey through feedback loops offers another way to encounter awareness: a story can let someone inhabit a question before they can express it formally.

Mathematics asks something different of the same curiosity. Natural phenomena have repeatedly prompted new mathematical descriptions. Causality is where I want to investigate the tools available for describing these relationships, their limits and the language we might still need.

The crossing between myths and mathematics matters to me. A myth can open an unfamiliar way of seeing; a mathematical account has to make its relationships precise enough to examine. Sleepwalker can explore the space between them without the story becoming proof of the scientific claim. Each form gives the inquiry something the other cannot supply on its own.

DAO Studio: the practical playground

DAO Studio is where the questions meet engineering and where an attractive account has to confront what can actually be made useful. Ideas from these different explorations can be put to work, and the next attempt can begin with something the previous one taught us. The abstract and the concrete keep meeting here.

A place to follow the crossings

getHarsh is the personal digital identity through which I catalogue and share these experiments. I think of it as decentralised in that the work has several homes: DAO Studio's practical work, Causality's research, Sleepwalker's fiction and RawThoughts' writing and discussion. Their connection is the inquiry travelling among them; each keeps its own purpose.

The loop comes back to the person asking the question. A practical difficulty can send me towards mathematics. A story can make a question vivid enough to pursue. An explanation can invite another person to notice what I missed. Publishing makes those crossings available for discussion, so that the next question need not come only from me.
