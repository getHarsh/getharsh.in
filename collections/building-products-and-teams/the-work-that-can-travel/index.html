  1. What belongs to the platform?

At Sachait Technologies Pvt. Ltd., I worked on reducing false alarms and turning detection into an emergency-response service. A model could flag an event, but somebody still had to inspect the evidence, decide what to do and hand the situation to another person. If the next handler could not see what had already been checked, the service could lose the benefit of the first person's work. The product had to carry the situation between people, not just send another notification.

I exited successfully through patent acquisition, with safe returns for investors and co-founders, and the work continued as YOJN Care at DAO Studio. The acquired IP concerned a patent application, not a granted patent. The emergency-response problem continued beyond the venture in which I had worked on it.

What travelled was more specific than an ambition to make people safer. Care's design connects detection to evidence, human review and response coordination. An operator who cannot obtain the evidence has a different problem from one who has inspected it and dismissed the alert. Someone taking over needs that distinction, along with what has already been attempted and what still needs attention. Carrying the mechanism forward means preserving those differences, which are easy to lose in a demonstration that ends when the notification arrives.

What belongs to the platform?

At DAO Studio we made three IP filings. I also built YojnAI, bringing models, tools, agents and checked playbooks into applications whose intermediate work people could inspect. The platform reached pilots in investment banking, operations and the CFO office. Here, useful work travelled through a shared mechanism into products with different owners and purposes.

YojnAI belonged to DAO Studio Private Limited, the company behind DAO Studio. The finance products built with it belonged to their partners. That arrangement let a team use the platform's composition and tracing capabilities without making its application part of the platform maker's own product catalogue. The application's documents, configuration and particular task still mattered: the same model call could be a small step in quite different work.

Suppose an application extracts the wrong reporting period from a document. A fluent final answer might conceal the mistake, especially if the arithmetic that follows is internally consistent. Being able to find the extraction gives the reviewer somewhere useful to intervene. The correction concerns a particular document and question; the means of locating that step can be useful in many applications.

YojnAI's improvement design followed the application and its version down to a trace and a step. Feedback could enter an improvement project, and a chosen change could be reviewed before being pushed to the application. The composition model gave that process objects to work with, rather than treating every correction as a reason to replace the whole system.

But reusing the tracing mechanism and reusing what it traced are different acts. Another application may need the same way to locate an extraction without having any use for the first customer's financial document, much less permission to take it. In YojnAI's arrangements, diagnostic and platform-improvement use of customer data required consent. Supplying the platform did not make the partners' products and records ours.

This is a different form of continuity from Sachait's technology becoming part of Care. A platform can remain with its maker while helping someone else's product develop. The reporting-period correction belongs to the task that needed it; the next application can still benefit from a mechanism that makes its own mistakes easier to find.
