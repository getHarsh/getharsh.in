# A question we can work on together

In community work, I keep returning to the distance between having an interesting conversation and finding something we can do together. One person wants to study learning, another is interested in a new architecture, a third wants to build something useful immediately. I recognise something of myself in each position. The interests may belong to the same broad field without yet belonging to the same piece of work.

I volunteer with and help lead [UPAI](https://upailabs.org/), the Uttar Pradesh AI community. Lucknow AI Labs, or LAI, is its founding Lucknow chapter. That association has given this question a practical setting. Helping a community grow and developing my own architectural ideas can happen in the same conversation, but they ask different things of the people listening. Someone may want to learn, offer a few hours, challenge the idea or simply observe. I do not want my enthusiasm for an architecture to decide which of those contributions counts.

[Learning with a real problem](https://getharsh.in/collections/learning-and-practice/a-real-problem-is-a-better-learning-brief/) gave my teaching a useful centre. I want shared research to have something similarly tangible: a question, an experiment or a stubborn result around which different people can contribute. The attraction is not merely distributing my plan among volunteers. It is finding an observation none of us could obtain merely by exchanging explanations.

<a id="making-the-question-small-changes-who-can-contribute"></a>

## Making the question small changes who can contribute

Time, interest and experience need separate attention. A person can know the subject and have little time; another can be a beginner with the patience to prepare examples carefully. Organisation, teaching, design and documentation also help research continue. Asking everybody to contribute code narrows the community before the question has even been chosen. I would rather find a piece of work small enough to carry, and clear enough that someone else can rely on it.

Consider an imagined group interested in whether retrieved context helps a language model use unfamiliar domain terms. “Build a system that understands the domain” is a large ambition. It leaves nearly every important choice open. A smaller question might ask whether short definitions improve answers to a fixed set of questions when the model and prompt are held constant. The result would not settle domain understanding. It would give the group a particular effect to investigate.

That reduction changes the work. Someone can read the relevant literature and clarify what previous comparisons actually measured. Someone can construct a small, permitted set of examples. Another person can implement the baseline and treatment. A reviewer can inspect whether the answers were judged consistently and whether the definitions accidentally contained the answers. These roles connect through the question. They are not separate personal projects placed under a common name.

It also gives disagreement somewhere useful to land. A participant might object that the questions are too easy, or that the definitions contain cues unavailable in ordinary use. The objection can improve the sample or narrow the claim. With a very broad ambition, the same disagreement often becomes an argument about the eventual architecture, long before the group has an observation to organise it.

A small question is not automatically an insignificant one. It can expose a dependency the larger ambition concealed. If the model succeeds only when the answer is effectively supplied in the context, the group has learned something different from successful transfer to new cases. If a simple baseline works equally well, a proposed mechanism may not yet have a useful job. The scale is modest enough that the disagreement can become an experiment.

<a id="contribution-needs-a-place-to-meet-another-contribution"></a>

## Contribution needs a place to meet another contribution

Giving each volunteer a task does not guarantee that their work will connect. The reader of a paper may extract a definition that the implementation never uses. The person creating examples may make assumptions the reviewer does not know. The person writing the script may change the input format and unintentionally change the experiment. Coordination depends on making those interfaces intelligible.

For the imagined comparison, that could mean agreeing on the question format, the source of the definitions and what an acceptable answer contains. Each contribution can then be inspected where it joins the next. A participant who cannot write the model code can still expose a bad comparison or an ambiguous example. The experiment becomes a shared object around which people can develop different abilities.

There is a practical limit to how many things a volunteer can carry reliably. Research needs reading, data work, review and communication as well as the visible implementation. Expanding the proposal consumes all of those capacities, often before it consumes additional compute. A plan that leaves every spare evening occupied has made itself fragile. A smaller, repeatable commitment lets others build on a contribution without requiring perpetual enthusiasm from the same person.

This is different from making the work easy. Research will still contain confusion and periods when the result is hard to interpret. The useful arrangement gives that difficulty continuity: someone can explain the current state, another can take a small piece and both can return to the same question. It makes room for an observer to begin contributing without pretending that joining the discussion assigns them responsibility for the whole undertaking.

<a id="the-artifact-lets-somebody-else-become-a-critic"></a>

## The artifact lets somebody else become a critic

A result explained only by the person who ran it cannot yet support much shared learning. The next participant needs enough of the configuration, inputs, method and output to understand what happened and try to challenge it. The relevant account is more specific than “the new approach worked.” It identifies what was changed and what was observed under that change.

The [NeurIPS paper checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist) asks for claims that match the contribution, experimental details and a reasonable way to reproduce or verify the result. Those questions are valuable before a paper exists. For our small group, they reveal whether the record is carrying the experiment or merely reporting enthusiasm about it. They do not require every research problem to use an identical release package.

The inputs matter as much as the script. The [Datasheets for Datasets proposal](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/datasheets-for-datasets/) connects a dataset's motivation, composition, collection and intended uses. Even a small sample benefits from knowing why these examples were selected and what they omit. Otherwise a later contributor can reuse a convenient file while quietly changing the meaning of the comparison.

Making an artifact inspectable is also a way of distributing intellectual responsibility. A newcomer can rerun a baseline, find a parsing problem or propose a counterexample. Their contribution need not begin with accepting the group's explanation. That is one reason reproducibility is educational as well as technical: it gives another person a place to encounter the claim through their own work.

<a id="a-shared-inquiry-can-change-direction-without-losing-itself"></a>

## A shared inquiry can change direction without losing itself

My own architectural exploration has changed as I have gone further into its foundations. A shared inquiry needs room for its explanation to change too. A community can help an idea become better precisely because it is not my team executing my product roadmap. Participants need enough of the question to disagree with it, and a way to make that disagreement useful without committing themselves to everything I want to investigate.

Suppose the comparison reveals that the definitions help familiar question forms but not a different phrasing. The group could respond by enlarging the system immediately. It could also keep the finding visible and investigate the dependence. That second movement may require less machinery and teach more. A question narrowed enough to begin should remain open enough to change when the result resists it.

There are many useful outcomes short of a novel paper: a faithful reproduction, a discovered error, an explanation of a limitation or a reusable evaluation case. They should be named for what they contribute. The ambition to produce new research can continue without treating every intermediate artifact as a new scientific result.

Credit needs a similarly careful account. Formulating the question, designing examples, implementing a comparison and interpreting its result are real contributions that can be made by different people. A community should make them legible rather than let the most visible speaker become the owner of everybody's work. That attention helps a participant understand both what they have contributed and what they still depend on others to do.

The deepest promise of shared research, for me, is that people can become more capable through the inquiry itself. The question gives them a common difficulty. The artifacts let the difficulty survive a particular conversation. Other people's criticism changes what each participant can see. A community begins to have continuity when that movement can happen again, even with a different person carrying the next part.
